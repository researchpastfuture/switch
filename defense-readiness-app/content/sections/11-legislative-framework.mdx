## Overview

This section presents the document's **legislative framework** — its argument that the doctrine's goals should be pursued through ordinary, lawful policymaking, and its proposal for the kinds of provisions such legislation might contain.

### Working through the legislative process

The authors are explicit that durable change runs through law. The framework they describe is oriented toward the standard process: drafting clear provisions, building support, and enacting and overseeing them through established institutions. References in the document to advocacy and political-action committees are presented in this conventional civic light — lawful participation in the policymaking process — and this edition presents them as the authors describe them.

### The Sample Bill Generator

The interactive **Sample Bill Generator** lets a reader assemble a model bill by selecting the provisions they want to include. The tool adds a short title and combines the chosen sections into formatted, copyable text. The provision types offered mirror the structure of typical legislation:

- **Definitions** (Section 1) — establishes the terms used in the Act.
- **Child protection mandate** (Section 2) — protective standards for minors, set by the responsible agency.
- **Reporting requirements** (Section 3) — defined incidents reported within a prescribed timeframe.
- **Funding authorization** (Section 4) — authorizes appropriations to carry out the Act.
- **Oversight & audit** (Section 5) — periodic audits and reporting of findings.
- **Enforcement** (Section 6) — penalties established by the responsible agency.

The generated text uses neutral, placeholder statutory language. It is a **template for learning and drafting conversations**, not actual legislation or legal advice; real lawmaking requires legislative counsel and the formal process.

### What the structure emphasizes

The set of provisions is itself instructive. The framework pairs a **child protection mandate** with **reporting requirements**, reflecting the document's recurring emphasis on both prevention and accountability. It pairs **funding authorization** with **oversight and audit**, echoing the SAFE-USA section's insistence that money be accountable. And it includes **enforcement** through defined penalties administered by a responsible agency rather than by private actors — consistent with the document's broader commitment to operating within lawful institutions.

Read together with the funding proposal (Section 10) and the metrics dashboard (Section 15), this section completes a loop the authors return to throughout: define the goal, fund it accountably, enact it lawfully, and measure whether it works.
