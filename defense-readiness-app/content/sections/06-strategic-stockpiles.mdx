## Overview

This section presents the document's case for **strategic stockpiles** and what it calls domestic arsenal zones — pre-positioned reserves of essential supplies intended to make communities resilient during emergencies and disruptions.

### The argument for reserves

The authors argue that resilience depends on having essential goods on hand before they are needed, rather than relying on supply chains that may fail under stress. The document frames stockpiling as a practical expression of preparedness: water, food, medical capacity, power, communications, and fuel, held in reserve at a community scale, reduce the consequences of any single disruption.

The emphasis is on **continuity and care** — keeping people supplied, connected, and treated during an emergency — rather than on confrontation. The supply categories the document highlights are oriented toward sustaining a population through a crisis.

### The Stockpile Estimator

The interactive **Stockpile Estimator** turns this into a planning aid. A reader enters the population to be served, and the tool calculates recommended quantities from editable per-capita ratios. The default ratios per 1,000 people are:

- **Emergency water reserve** — 3,000 gallons
- **Shelf-stable meals** — 9,000 meals
- **Trauma / medical kits** — 40 kits
- **Backup generators** — 2 units
- **Field radios** — 15 units
- **Reserve fuel** — 500 gallons

These figures are starting points the document offers for discussion, not official standards. Because the ratios live in an editable data file, a planner can adjust them to match local guidance, climate, or risk profile, and the estimates update accordingly.

### How the document frames implementation

The authors present stockpiling as something that scales from the household up to the regional level, and they tie it to the broader doctrine in two ways. First, they connect reserves to the funding mechanism proposed later (the SAFE-USA fund in Section 10), arguing that durable preparedness needs a dedicated and accountable source of support. Second, they connect it to the metrics framework (Section 15), where "strategic stockpile coverage" appears as a benchmark to track over time.

Throughout, the document treats stockpiling as **lawful, civic preparedness** in the tradition of emergency-management planning — coordinated with established authorities, sized to real needs, and reviewed regularly so that reserves remain current and usable rather than forgotten.
