## Overview

This section presents the document's **Watchtower Model** — its proposal for combining open-source intelligence (OSINT) and human intelligence (HUMINT) into a layered "national shield." The authors argue that awareness, drawn from many ordinary and lawful sources, is itself a form of protection.

### Two kinds of intelligence

The document draws a standard distinction and builds on it:

- **OSINT (open-source intelligence)** — information gathered from publicly available sources: open reporting and published filings, public social-media signals, openly accessible forums, and searchable official registries.
- **HUMINT (human intelligence)** — information gathered through people and relationships: community tip lines, trusted faith and civic liaisons, and on-the-ground field observers.

The authors argue that neither layer is sufficient alone. Open sources provide breadth and can be checked by anyone; human relationships provide context and judgment that raw data lacks. The "watchtower" image is meant to convey overlapping vantage points rather than a single all-seeing eye.

### Composing coverage

The interactive **Watchtower Coverage Builder** lets readers toggle individual sources on and off and see how many of five categories — public web, social, community, field reports, and records — are covered by the current selection. The point the tool illustrates is the document's argument that resilience comes from **diversity of sources**: relying on one channel leaves blind spots, while a balanced mix closes gaps.

### Lawful and rights-respecting by design

This is a sensitive area, and the document is explicit about its limits. As presented here, the Watchtower Model is built on **publicly available information and voluntary community relationships**. It is framed as a tool for situational awareness and for routing concerns to the proper authorities — not for surveilling specific individuals, and not as a substitute for law enforcement or due process. The authors emphasize that any intelligence function must operate within the law and in cooperation with established institutions, and that respect for privacy and civil liberties is a condition of its legitimacy rather than an obstacle to it.

### Why the document frames awareness as a shield

The authors argue that many harms they describe earlier — exploitation, trafficking, predatory influence — depend on going unnoticed. Their proposed remedy is distributed attention: a community in which open information is monitored responsibly and in which residents know how to report what they see. In this framing, the "shield" is less a centralized agency than a culture of informed, lawful vigilance, with the technical and human pieces arranged so that no single category of insight is missing.
