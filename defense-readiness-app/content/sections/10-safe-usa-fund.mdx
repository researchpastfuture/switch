## Overview

This section explains the document's proposed funding mechanism: the **SAFE-USA fund** — the Strategic Arsenal & Freedom Emergency Fund. The authors argue that the doctrine's other proposals require a dedicated, accountable source of support, and SAFE-USA is presented as that source.

### Why a dedicated fund

A recurring argument in the document is that preparedness fails when it depends on year-to-year improvisation. The authors contend that durable capacity — stockpiles that stay current, programs that continue, rescue and recovery work that is sustained — needs a stable funding stream with clear priorities. SAFE-USA is framed as a way to make the doctrine's commitments concrete and to keep them from eroding over time.

### The allocation simulator

The interactive **SAFE-USA Allocation Simulator** lets a reader distribute a notional 100% of the fund across the priorities the document identifies, with sliders and a live total. The default allocation the document offers is:

- **Child rescue & recovery — 30%**
- **School shields — 20%**
- **Strategic stockpiles — 15%**
- **Watchtower / intelligence — 15%**
- **Recovery programs — 10%**
- **Emergency reserve — 10%**

The tool flags whether the selections add up to 100%, making the trade-offs visible: increasing one priority requires reducing another. These percentages are illustrative starting points for discussion, not a budget proposal with force; the simulator's value is in helping readers reason about priorities for themselves.

### What the allocation reveals

The default split is itself an expression of the document's priorities. **Child rescue and recovery receives the largest share**, consistent with the authors' framing of child protection as the doctrine's central concern. **Recovery programs and an emergency reserve** each receive a dedicated portion, reflecting the document's emphasis on aftercare and on keeping capacity in hand for the unexpected. The remaining priorities map directly to earlier sections — schools, stockpiles, and the watchtower model — so the fund reads as a financial summary of the whole doctrine.

### Accountability

The document frames SAFE-USA as a public, accountable instrument rather than an open-ended one. This emphasis connects to the legislative framework in Section 11, where funding authorization is paired with oversight and audit provisions, and to the metrics dashboard in Section 15, which the authors present as a way to judge whether spending is producing the intended results. As with the rest of the document, the proposal is offered for examination and debate, and the figures are starting points readers are invited to test and revise.
